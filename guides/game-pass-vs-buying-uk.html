Game Pass vs buying games: a UK guide

Published 14 October 2026. UK prices taken from the official Xbox store on that date; Microsoft can change them at any time.

Xbox Game Pass is Microsoft's subscription that gives you access to a library of games for a monthly fee, a bit like Netflix for games. Whether it's better value than buying games depends almost entirely on how you play. Here's what it costs in the UK right now and how to decide.

The real difference: renting vs owning

With Game Pass you're renting access. Games join and leave the library, and when your subscription stops you can no longer play them (your saves usually remain, and Microsoft offers member discounts if you want to buy a game outright). When you buy a game, you keep the licence for as long as the store exists.

A simple break-even test

Work out how many games you'd realistically buy in a year and what you'd pay for them. PC Game Pass at £10.99 a month is £131.88 a year if you subscribe all year. If you'd only buy two or three discounted games in that time, buying is probably cheaper, especially if you shop in sales (see our guide to cheap PC games). If you'd otherwise buy several new full-price releases, or you like trying lots of games you wouldn't pay for, the subscription can come out well ahead.

Game Pass tends to suit you if…

  • you like sampling lots of different games rather than replaying a few favourites
  • you want big new Xbox-published releases on day one (PC Game Pass or Ultimate)
  • you play on both an Xbox and a PC, or want cloud play on a phone or TV (Ultimate)
  • you're happy to subscribe for a month or two, binge, then cancel

Buying tends to suit you if…

  • you mostly play one or two games for months at a time
  • you like waiting for sales and building a permanent library
  • you play lots of games that aren't in the library, which includes most games from Sony, Nintendo, Valve and many independent publishers
  • you collect freebies from the Epic Games Store and only buy occasionally

Tips for getting the most from Game Pass

  • Turn off auto-renew if you're bingeing. You can do this at account.microsoft.com/services and still keep access until the end of the period you've paid for.
  • Check "leaving soon". The Xbox app flags games about to leave the library so you can finish them first.
  • Know the refund rules. Microsoft says you can get a refund within 30 days of your first purchase by cancelling, and, once per subscription, a refund of your most recent recurring charge if you cancel and ask within 30 days of being charged.
  • Watch for price changes. Microsoft must give at least 30 days' notice of a price increase, so you have time to cancel.

Our take

For a PC player who tries lots of new games, a month or two of PC Game Pass now and then is excellent value. For someone who plays a handful of games deeply, buying in sales and claiming free games usually wins. Many people mix both: subscribe when there's a run of games they want, cancel in between, and buy the ones they want to keep forever.
